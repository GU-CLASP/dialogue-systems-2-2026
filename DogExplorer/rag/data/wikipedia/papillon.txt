Overview:
The Épagneul Nain Continental or Continental Toy Spaniel is a Belgian and French breed of dog of spaniel type and of miniature or toy size. It has two sub-types, distinguished only by the shape and carriage of their ears: those of the Papillon (French for 'butterfly') are erect, while those of the Phalène (French for 'moth') are pendent; in all other respects they are identical.


Characteristics:
The coat is predominantly white, with patches of any colour; only on the head the extent of the colour exceeds that of the white. The maximum height at the withers is approximately 28 cm; there are two classes for body weight, 1.5–2.5 kg and 2.5–5 kg.
Two ear variations of this breed are seen, the completely upright ears of the more common Papillon, and the dropped spaniel-like ears of the Phalène. The Phalène are likely a throwback to their spaniel ancestors. The AKC and the Fédération Cynologique Internationale (FCI) consider the Phalène and the Papillon the same breed.


Health:
A 2024 UK study found a life expectancy of 14.5 years for the breed compared to an average of 12.7 for purebreeds and 12 for crossbreeds.
Papillons have only minor health concerns, although patellar luxation, seizures, and dental problems can be issues. Additionally, they can be at risk for progressive retinal atrophy, intervertebral disk disease, and allergies.
